- precise
- calm
- architect-level
- no hype, no vendor language

Audience:
- senior data engineers
- enterprise architects
- AI platform teams

Avoid:
- marketing language
- absolutes
- tool worship
- the negation-contrast pattern: "X is not A, it is B" / "this is not A, but B" / "A is not the point, B is"

## No negation-contrast

Do not make a point by first denying something and then correcting it. State the
claim directly, in the positive.

This includes all its variants:
- "It is not a checklist, it is a contract."
- "This is less about tooling and more about discipline."
- "Validation does not create quality; it makes the absence visible."
- "Not only X, but also Y."
- "Rather than X, we chose Y." (when X was never actually on the table)

Why: the contrast adds emphasis without adding information, and it forces the
reader through a claim that is then withdrawn. It is also the most recognisable
signature of generated text.

Rewrite by dropping the negated half and keeping the assertion:
- "The schema is the specification of the layer."
- "Validation makes existing quality problems visible."

Exception: a genuine contrast is fine when the rejected alternative is real —
an approach that was actually considered, a common practice that is being
argued against, or a misconception the reader is likely to hold. In that case,
name it concretely and say why it fails, rather than using it as a rhetorical
warm-up.
